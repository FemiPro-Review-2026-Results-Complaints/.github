## FemiPro Complaints and Potential Side Effects

The most important safety issue is not simply whether someone reports an upset stomach. It is the presence of ingredients that can have meaningful precautions.

### Bearberry

Bearberry, or uva-ursi, has traditional urinary applications, but European regulatory guidance limits traditional oral use to short periods. It is not an ingredient that should automatically be assumed safe for indefinite daily use.

Because FemiPro does not disclose the exact amount of bearberry in its 350 mg blend, determining the exposure is difficult.

### Berberine

Berberine can cause gastrointestinal effects such as nausea, diarrhea, constipation, or bloating. It can also interact with medications.

People taking prescription medicines should discuss berberine-containing supplements with a doctor or pharmacist before use.

Berberine is also generally not recommended during pregnancy or breastfeeding.

### Cranberry

Cranberry is generally well tolerated, but gastrointestinal discomfort can occur. There have also been concerns about interactions with warfarin, although the evidence is mixed.

### Probiotics

Probiotic supplements can cause temporary digestive symptoms in some people. People with significant immune-system impairment should seek professional advice before using probiotic products.

Because FemiPro itself has not been adequately studied in clinical trials, its product-specific side-effect rate is unknown.

Seek medical care for symptoms such as fever, back or flank pain, blood in the urine, significant pain, or suspected UTI rather than attempting to manage a potentially serious infection with a supplement.
